## 2026-01-15 10:30 — Economic life

**Topic:** Why do housing markets feel political rather than merely economic?

**Domains:** Economics, political science, history

**Conversation:**

Housing is partly an economic market: prices and rents respond to supply, demand, credit conditions, expected returns, and regulation. But it becomes political because public decisions shape the market's basic rules: zoning, social housing, tax treatment, tenant protections, infrastructure, and access to mortgage finance.

The historical lens adds an important caution. The connection between ownership, security, and citizenship is not universal or timeless; it emerged through particular welfare systems, legal regimes, and political coalitions. This means neither an economic model nor an appeal to "culture" is enough on its own.

**Possible callback:** Compare housing with education: both allocate scarce opportunities through institutions that appear private but have public consequences.

**Sources to verify for a live session:** local housing statistics, relevant legislation, and credible historical scholarship for the country or period under discussion.
