# Worldview Mentor

**One mentor for connected, revisable learning across history, economics, politics, sociology, philosophy, literature, art, and film.**

Most AI learning sessions are useful in isolation but forget the larger picture. Worldview Mentor helps you investigate a concrete question through several appropriate lenses, preserve the important differences between them, and return to earlier ideas only when a new topic makes the connection genuinely useful.

It is a Codex skill for people who want a durable model of the world, not a collection of disconnected subject tutors or an ever-growing pile of notes.

## A concrete example

**Question:** *Why do housing markets feel political rather than merely economic?*

Worldview Mentor would not simply give three separate explainers. It would:

1. build the economic model: supply constraints, credit, rents, and distribution;
2. introduce the political question: who can change the rules, and who benefits from the existing ones;
3. add a historical lens: how home ownership became associated with security, citizenship, and social status in particular places and periods;
4. distinguish evidence, scholarly interpretation, and normative judgment; and
5. retain only the durable connection for a future conversation on inequality, urban planning, or democratic legitimacy.

The goal is not to make every topic look connected. It is to make the *right* connections legible, including their limits.

## What it does

- Leads recurring or ad-hoc sessions through a shared set of disciplinary lenses.
- Starts from concrete objects: an event, institution, work of art, policy, passage, social practice, or lived dilemma.
- Separates facts, interpretations, inferences, and value judgments.
- Recalls earlier concepts only when they add a real comparison, counterexample, or unresolved tension.
- Supports an optional, low-clutter Obsidian knowledge base.
- Verifies changing, disputed, historical, political, economic, scholarly, and work-specific claims against reliable sources.

## What makes it different

Interdisciplinary learning is not the same as saying that everything is connected.

Worldview Mentor treats disciplines as distinct lenses on shared questions: institutions, incentives, power, social structure, historical change, values, and lived experience. An economic model is not a complete political explanation; a philosophical concept does not settle an empirical question; an aesthetic response is not reducible to sociology. The skill is designed to preserve those differences while making comparison possible.

## What it will not do

- Create a daily note, a flashcard, or a new folder for every conversation.
- Force superficial links between unrelated topics.
- Treat a model, ideology, or preference as a universal explanation.
- Overwrite a user's notes or infer a private knowledge-base structure without approval.
- Spoil major literary, film, or dramatic endings unless asked.

## Install

Copy this directory into your Codex skills directory:

```text
~/.codex/skills/worldview-mentor/
```

Then invoke it with `$worldview-mentor`, or let Codex select it for a relevant cross-disciplinary learning request.

On first use, the skill asks whether you want a knowledge base and where it should live. It does not assume a private path, a schedule, or any personal notes.

## Optional Obsidian knowledge base

The suggested structure deliberately uses only three durable notes:

| Note | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `Worldview Project.md` | Goals, weekly rhythm, boundaries, and continuation instructions. |
| `Worldview Library.md` | One continuous record of substantive conversations. |
| `Cross-disciplinary Theme Map.md` | A curated map of concepts that recur meaningfully across domains. |

See the anonymous, copyable examples in [`examples/obsidian-vault`](examples/obsidian-vault). They contain no personal notes or user data.

## Who it is for

This skill is useful for self-directed learners, educators, writers, researchers, and anyone who wants intellectual continuity across fields. It works especially well for people who enjoy following a question across disciplines but do not want to build and maintain a complex knowledge-management system themselves.

It is not intended as a substitute for disciplinary expertise, primary research, professional advice, or a complete academic curriculum.
